# 3 Wins from 5 Hackathons — My Coding Journey & Leadership Experience

I've participated in **5 hackathons** during my B.Tech journey. Won **3** of them, secured a major 4th place finish at a national stage, and failed at one. Here is how it unfolded, along with how I started our college's Design Club.

---

## The First Hackathon — Failed (Too Much Hurry)

In my first hackathon, my team and I were in a massive hurry. We rushed through the planning phase, started coding immediately without structuring the database schema properly, and tried to build way too many features at once. 

Because of the rush, our prototype crashed during the final testing phase, and we couldn't deliver a functional MVP. 

**Lesson learned:** Rushing without a clear roadmap leads to failure. Take time to plan before writing the first line of code.

---

## The Second Hackathon — Won

Having learned from our first failure, we paced ourselves. We defined a clear, realistic scope and spent the first 4 hours focusing entirely on layout, user flow, and architecture. 

Our prototype was extremely clean, fully functional, and addressed a real-world problem with an intuitive UI. The judges loved the usability and structure.

**Lesson learned:** A simple, polished, and fully working product beats an incomplete complex one.

---

## The Third Hackathon — Won

By the third hackathon, we had developer chemistry down to a science. I took ownership of the real-time websocket components and frontend UI rendering. 

We focused on technical depth and performance optimization, presenting a bulletproof live demo that handled simulated concurrent traffic flawlessly.

**Lesson learned:** Focus on your strengths, automate your presentation slides, and leave ample time for live QA preparation.

---

## The Fourth Hackathon — 4th Place at IIT Kanpur

This was a major national-level hackathon held at **IIT Kanpur**. The competition was fierce, with teams from top institutes across India. 

While we didn't take home the top trophy, our AI-powered decision support prototype ranked **4th place nationally**. It was an incredible validation of our engineering standards and gave us massive exposure.

**Lesson learned:** Competing against the best pushes your skills to a completely new level.

---

## The Fifth Hackathon — Won

Our fifth hackathon was the culmination of everything we learned. We combined high-end UX design, scalable backend integration, and a clear business use-case. 

We kept our cool, managed our time perfectly, and delivered a seamless product presentation that won over the entire judging panel.

**Lesson learned:** Consistency, experience, and keeping a cool head under pressure is the winning formula.

---

## Building the Design Club — From Zero to Initiative

Alongside hackathons, I noticed that most CS students at our college had no outlet for creative design thinking. So I started the **Design Club**.

It started with 4 people in a classroom. I ran the first session myself — teaching Figma basics and explaining why layout design matters for developers.

Within a semester, we had 30+ active members running weekly Figma workshops, UI/UX critique sessions, and collaborative hackathon preparation.

**What I initiated:**
- Structured the club's curriculum from scratch
- Organized the first inter-college design challenge
- Mentored junior students on Figma, typography, and visual hierarchy
